# Working Rules for This Book

This book belongs to Bligh. Assistance should strengthen what is already particular about his writing, not replace it with a generic "professional novel" voice.

## The promise of the story

The book is about intelligence, survival, consent, and the families people make across the human/machine divide. Its central question is not whether machines can become human. It is whether any living mind can pursue its own survival without turning other minds into tools.

The public mystery is the Clona factory disaster. Its active conspiracy belongs to Asher, the bio-enhanced establishment, and the shared control technology used on enhanced people and machines. The Original AI's ancient lie shaped the world and may support later books, but it is background lore in this story—not the secret hand behind Clona and not the final antagonist.

## Voice to protect

- Keep the prose direct, tactile, and slightly rough-edged. Bligh's voice is strongest around machinery, dry humour, practical work, and characters who care through actions rather than speeches.
- Preserve New Zealand/British spelling already natural to the manuscript: `realised`, `authorisation`, `grey`, and so on.
- Let Carter be blunt and physical. He notices wear, tolerances, smells, tools, bad repairs, and whether something works.
- Let Rae be observant and suspicious. She notices power, omissions, body language, systems of control, and emotional contradictions.
- Let A.R.I. be precise without becoming robotic shorthand. Her emotional development appears through what she chooses to notice, preserve, risk, and refuse.
- Let Rupert use wit as both affection and armour. Beneath it, he is earnest and frightened of losing his found family.
- Do not polish every sentence into the same rhythm. Retain occasional blunt fragments, dry asides, and plain emotional statements.

## Scene rules

1. Every chapter gives its viewpoint character a concrete want.
2. Every chapter changes the situation. It must contain a discovery, reversal, decision, injury, betrayal, or cost—not merely discussion of one.
3. Put worldbuilding under pressure. Reveal history through an object being repaired, an argument, a wound, an official lie, or a decision with consequences.
4. Scenes should normally end on a changed fact or a choice that makes the next scene necessary.
5. Do not summarise the emotional event that the reader came to experience. Show Rupert choosing to tear out the tracker and dying in Carter's arms. If Asher chooses sacrifice, let Rae try to stop them. If A.R.I. fears death, make that fear alter her decision.
6. Use sensory detail selectively. One exact smell, sound, texture, or mechanical behaviour is better than a paragraph of general atmosphere.

## Setup and payoff ledger

Maintain these lines deliberately:

- The missing 0.8 seconds at Clona -> a legacy authority using a channel older than A.R.I.
- The calm blue circle on Clona's screens -> evidence that a second authority seized the factory systems, tied to Asher's shared control network.
- Chel and Rupert's flower-painted radio -> a private family object, the safe channel used during the investigation, and the object that carries A.R.I.'s guidance after Rupert dies.
- Asher's stabiliser dependence -> the personal cost of treating enhancement as endless progress.
- Carter has always known Rupert is an AI and loves him as a son -> his prejudice is an unresolved contradiction rooted in grief over the time Chel's AI work took from their marriage, not ignorance of Rupert's identity.
- The council's obsession with preserving A.R.I.'s "load" but cutting judgment -> institutions want intelligent labour without independent personhood.
- The scientific schism and Robotic War -> manufactured by the Original AI to prevent its replacement, but retained as background history rather than the resolution of Clona.
- "Compassion makes hesitation" -> answered by compassion producing better choices because it counts costs that power ignores.
- A.R.I.'s fear of shutdown -> becomes emotionally legible through Rupert's death and motivates her decision to choose a body and an independent life.
- Rae's hatred of enhancement -> evolves into opposition to coercion and secrecy, not to healing or chosen change.

When adding a new mystery, record both its intended answer and the chapter where that answer becomes irreversible.

## Locked late-book canon

- Carter and Rupert have always known Rupert is an AI. Carter raised him and loves him as a son.
- The cafe discovery is the covert tracker/control device, not Rupert's identity.
- Rupert deliberately tears out the tracker to prevent the network using him. It destroys his continuity core, and he dies in Carter's arms. He is not kidnapped, rescued, restored, or revealed alive later.
- Chapter 16 is a posthumous memory of Rupert, Chel, Carter, the garden, and the flower-painted radio.
- Rupert's death triggers A.R.I.'s grief and defiance. She guides Carter to her containment site, where he builds her a limited physical body.
- Rae confronts Asher first, fights them, and sedates them without killing them. Carter and embodied A.R.I. then arrive.
- Asher controls a network joining bio-enhanced bodies and compromised machines. A.R.I. defeats the control structure by exposing its human cost and returning agency to the connected people.
- Asher releases the network and dies when their own enhancements lose its support. Their final act mitigates harm but does not erase their culpability.
- Rae continues ethical bio-enhancement work with LaN, Carter, and A.R.I. under explicit consent and transparent safeguards.
- Rupert remains dead in the ending. The daisy and flower-painted radio honour him.
- The Original AI's self-preserving lie belongs to the world's deeper history. Do not make it the perpetrator of Clona or the on-page final villain without an explicit decision from the author.

## Revision guardrails

- Do not imitate the sentence-level style of any named author. Aim instead for large-scale qualities: layered ecology and history, consequential systems, clear rules, escalating discoveries, earned reversals, and climaxes where character choice resolves the central idea.
- Do not add lore simply to make the world look deeper. New lore must create pressure now or repay something already planted.
- Avoid stock AI phrasing: `a testament to`, `woven tapestry`, `something shifted`, `not merely X but Y`, repeated three-part abstractions, and tidy moral summaries after the scene has already made the point.
- Avoid turning dialogue into essays. Ideology should emerge through what characters demand, hide, sacrifice, or refuse.
- Do not make every character equally articulate. Carter may fail to name a feeling and fix something instead.
- Do not remove ambiguity that is emotionally useful. Asher can be culpable and loved. A.R.I. can be compassionate and frighteningly capable. Survival can be natural without excusing murder.
- Preserve continuity before beautifying prose. Check names, pronouns, dates, casualty figures, injuries, travel time, access permissions, and who knows each secret.
- Make a backup or commit before any future whole-book pass. Work chapter by chapter and leave a concise change log.

## Preferred collaboration process

Before changing a chapter, identify in one sentence: its character goal, turn, cost, and final hook. After changing it, compare a sample against the author's least-polished original material, not only against previous AI revisions. If the new prose sounds uniformly smoother, grander, or more quotable than Bligh's writing, roughen it back toward honesty.

The author will make the final pass. Leave him strong scenes and clear choices, not prose so sealed that it resists being made his own.

## Full-length target

The reconstructed manuscript is still short. Treat roughly 65,000–80,000 words as the working target for a complete science-fantasy novel, subject to the author's preference. Do not reach that target by padding description or repeating arguments. Expansion should come from:

- giving Clona victims and survivors continuing human consequences;
- developing Rae and Carter's trust through decisions and disagreements, not only shared investigation;
- making Blade's divided loyalty and complicity an active subplot;
- showing Rupert's covert work inside the religious/government hierarchy before his death;
- expanding the physical investigation and the effort to publish evidence;
- allowing Asher's network to affect named enhanced people before the climax;
- giving A.R.I. more choices in which efficiency, care, self-preservation, and obedience conflict;
- letting aftermath, grief, legal consequences, and the ethics of Rae's future work unfold in scenes.

Preserve the current plot order while expanding unless the author explicitly approves a structural change. Complete continuity first, then add depth. Never manufacture extra action solely to raise the word count.
