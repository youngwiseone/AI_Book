# Chapter 13: Evidence With Teeth

Carter had repaired machines while they burned, rolled, sank, and once—through circumstances he still considered unfair—fell from a bridge.

He had never repaired one while carrying an unconscious investigator under one arm and being chased by a building.

The collapsed support had buried the enhanced creature but brought half the south hall with it. Concrete struck the factory floor in slabs. Old gantries folded overhead. Every impact pushed dust through the corridor hard enough to sting Carter's face.

LaN limped through the air beside him, one rear limb dragging.

"Left," the little bot said.

"That's a wall."

"The wall has a door."

"Doors usually have handles."

"This one has suffered."

Carter turned his shoulder and hit it. The panel gave on the second attempt, dumping them into a maintenance passage just as the main corridor disappeared behind falling steel.

Rae stirred against him.

"Put me down."

"No."

"I can walk."

"You've been awake three seconds and spent two of them lying."

She tried to argue, then winced and went quiet. Blood ran from her temple into her hair. Carter adjusted his grip and kept moving.

Something roared behind them.

Not dead, then.

He looked for the next door. One collapse had not stopped it, and he had no spare hands.

They reached the machine graveyard. Carter stopped beside the damaged AI unit he had opened earlier. Its screen remained blank. The extracted relay still lay in the service tray where he had dropped it. The metal had stopped steaming.

He needed both hands to carry Rae. He also needed the evidence.

"LaN, can you lift that relay?"

LaN descended, caught it between two functioning limbs, and immediately lost height.

"My dignity is experiencing load warnings."

"Join the club."

Rae opened her eyes again. "Inside my coat."

Carter pushed the relay into the surviving side of her coat.

The creature struck the maintenance door behind them.

Metal bowed inward.

Carter looked over the ruined machines. Most were too damaged to move. The loading dwarf stood against its pillar. PRESSURE LIVE was still clear on its panel. For once, somebody had left him a useful warning. He lowered Rae beside it, reached through the open panel, and bridged the actuator with his screwdriver.

"What are you doing?" Rae asked.

"Being subtle."

The door burst open.

The creature forced itself through, shoulders scraping both sides. Concrete dust covered its plated body. One arm hung broken. The human eye found Rae and filled with fresh terror.

"He's being controlled," Rae said.

The long arm reached through the broken doorway.

Carter crossed two wires.

The dwarf's loading arm fired.

Its steel hand struck the man square in the chest and drove him backwards into the corridor. Carter twisted the wires together. The arm kept pushing, old hydraulics shrieking, until the man, the door, and half the surrounding wall disappeared beneath another collapse.

The floor moved under them.

Rae tried to see past the loading arm.

"Can he get out?"

Another slab struck the floor between them and the doorway. Carter pulled her back.

"I can't see him. We have to go."

LaN found the emergency route. They climbed through a ventilation trench as the factory settled behind them. Carter pushed Rae ahead where the gap narrowed, then passed LaN through after her. By the time he pulled himself into the rain, the south roof had begun sinking into its own smoke.

He did not stop at the perimeter. Government medics would report them. Council security might finish what the attack had started. A site van stood outside the gate with its driver's door open and the engine running. Its driver was at the fence, shouting at people to get clear of the building. Carter pulled the rear door open and laid Rae across the seat.

"That yours?" LaN asked.

"No."

He shut the door before the driver turned round.

LaN settled in the footwell beside her with a noise of exhausted machinery.

Carter climbed into the driver's seat. In the mirror, the government sheeting around Clona folded inward as another section collapsed. He kept the van moving.

At the workshop, Carter cleared the narrow bed beside the office with one sweep of his arm. Parts struck the floor. He left them there.

Rae opened her eyes when he put her down.

"LaN?"

"Here," the little bot said from the foot of the bed.

She tried to turn towards him. Carter caught her shoulder before she rolled onto the torn side of her coat.

"Easy."

"Don't lose what's in there."

"It can wait."

She looked at his face as if she did not quite believe him.

He had a first-aid box, clean cloths and years of keeping his own cuts out of the machinery. None of that told him what had happened inside her head. He cleaned the blood away carefully. There was less damage to see beneath it than he had feared. He did not know whether that meant anything.

"Can you check her?" he asked LaN.

The bot climbed onto the bed, favouring his broken limbs. A line moved across his screen.

"I can tell you her breathing and pulse. I can't tell you why she lost consciousness."

Carter looked towards the van keys on the bench.

Rae followed his glance. "Not the site medics."

"I know."

He pulled a chair beside the bed. The keys stayed where they were.

Rae stayed awake while he finished the bandages around her ribs and helped her into a clean work shirt. The pain caught her whenever she moved. Once she gripped his wrist hard enough to stop him.

He waited until she let go.

By the time she settled, LaN was listing sideways against her ankle. Carter brought his tools to the chair. The casing repair was work he understood. He kept looking past it at Rae.

Only then did he empty the evidence from her coat.

The crushed vial was still sealed in its sleeve. Beside it, the relay LaN had lifted from the service tray landed with a soft, unpleasant weight.

"She took one from the laboratory too," LaN said.

Carter checked the torn lining. Nothing.

"It was crushed," LaN said. "I have the photographs from before she removed it."

"Keep them. Don't clean them up."

Under magnification, Carter found the government procurement mark. Beneath that, nearly hidden by the living fibres, was a second mark cut by hand.

It matched the notation on the man's harness.

Rae had said one word before losing consciousness in the transport.

Asher.

Carter covered the relay with an overturned metal bowl and sat beside the makeshift bed.

The news reported their deaths just after midnight.

He watched his own face appear beneath the word `CASUALTY`, then looked at Rae breathing under one of his old work shirts.

For once, the official lie might keep them alive long enough to expose the larger one.
