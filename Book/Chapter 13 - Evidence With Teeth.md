# Chapter 13: Evidence With Teeth

Carter had repaired machines while they burned, rolled, sank, and once—through circumstances he still considered unfair—fell from a bridge.

He had never repaired one while carrying an unconscious investigator under one arm and being chased by a building.

The collapsed support had buried the enhanced creature but brought half the south hall with it. Concrete struck the factory floor in slabs. Old gantries folded overhead. Every impact pushed dust through the corridor hard enough to sting Carter's face.

LaN limped through the air beside him, one rear limb dragging.

"Left," the little bot said.

"That's a wall."

"The wall has a door."

"Doors usually have handles."

"This one has suffered."

Carter turned his shoulder and hit it. The panel gave on the second attempt, dumping them into a maintenance passage just as the main corridor disappeared behind falling steel.

Rae stirred against him.

"Put me down."

"No."

"I can walk."

"You've been awake three seconds and spent two of them lying."

She tried to argue, then winced and went quiet. Blood ran from her temple into her hair. Carter adjusted his grip and kept moving.

Something roared behind them.

Not dead, then.

He had not expected it to be. Anything built with that much contempt for ordinary anatomy would be difficult to kill and probably impossible to reason with. The thought reminded him of several council officials.

They reached the machine graveyard. Carter stopped beside the damaged AI unit he had opened earlier. Its screen remained blank. The foreign components lay where he had left them, half-disconnected from the original circuitry.

He needed both hands to carry Rae. He also needed the evidence.

"LaN, can you lift that relay?"

LaN descended, caught it between two functioning limbs, and immediately lost height.

"My dignity is experiencing load warnings."

"Join the club."

Rae opened her eyes again. "Inside my coat."

Carter pushed the relay into the surviving side of her coat. His fingers caught on something wet and fibrous along the component.

Tissue.

Not residue from the creature. It had been grown through the machine's control bridge, living material wrapped around a hardware instruction. Rae's evidence and his were not separate. Whoever built the enhanced attacker had used the same method to force false data into the factory AIs.

The creature struck the maintenance door behind them.

Metal bowed inward.

Carter looked over the ruined machines. Most were too damaged to move. One old loading dwarf still had pressure in its left arm. He lowered Rae beside it, pulled the access plate, and bridged the actuator with his screwdriver.

"What are you doing?" Rae asked.

"Being subtle."

The door burst open.

The creature forced itself through, shoulders scraping both sides. Concrete dust covered its plated body. One arm hung broken. The human eye found Rae and filled with fresh terror.

Carter crossed two wires.

The dwarf's loading arm fired.

Its steel hand struck the creature square in the chest and drove it backwards into the corridor. Carter twisted the wires together. The arm kept pushing, old hydraulics shrieking, until creature, door, and half the surrounding wall disappeared beneath another collapse.

The floor moved under them.

"That," Rae said weakly, "was your subtle plan?"

"It noticed, didn't it?"

LaN found the emergency route. They climbed through a ventilation trench as the factory settled behind them. Carter pushed Rae ahead where the gap narrowed, then passed LaN through after her. By the time he pulled himself into the rain, the south roof had begun sinking into its own smoke.

He did not stop at the perimeter. Government medics would report them. Council security might finish what the creature had started. He carried Rae past the outer road to his transport and laid her across the back seat.

LaN docked beside her with a noise of exhausted machinery.

Carter climbed into the driver's seat. In the mirror, the government sheeting around Clona folded inward as another section collapsed. Anyone watching would believe Rae and Carter remained inside.

That could be useful.

At the workshop, he cleaned Rae's head wound, bound her ribs, and repaired the worst break in LaN's casing. Only then did he empty the evidence from her coat.

The wetware relay landed on the bench with a soft, unpleasant weight.

Under magnification, Carter found the government procurement mark. Beneath that, nearly hidden by the living fibres, was a second mark cut by hand.

It matched the notation on the creature's harness.

Rae had said one word before losing consciousness in the transport.

Asher.

Carter covered the relay with an overturned metal bowl and sat beside the makeshift bed.

The news reported their deaths just after midnight.

He watched his own face appear beneath the word `CASUALTY`, then looked at Rae breathing under one of his old work shirts.

For once, the official lie might keep them alive long enough to expose the larger one.
