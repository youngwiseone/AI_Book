# Chapter 10: The Graveyard of Machines

The disposal hall beneath Clona looked like a place machines had gone to be punished.

Service units lay in rows between concrete pillars. Some carried evidence tags. Others had been thrown into heaps high enough that lower bodies bent beneath the weight. Outer shells had been opened, memory housings removed, and identity plates stacked in a bin near the door.

Carter swept his lamp across them.

"Search team did this?"

No one answered, which was normal when he addressed a room full of dead things.

He walked between the rows. Clona's emergency units had been designed to enter fire, lift wreckage, and guide workers through smoke. During the disaster, most had done none of those things. The public report blamed A.R.I.'s command failure.

The first unit Carter opened had no command receiver.

Somebody had removed it after the blast.

The second had been burned so thoroughly that the chassis came apart under his screwdriver. The third still held its original systems, but an unfamiliar bridge had been fitted around them: a thin ring of dark metal threaded with pale living tissue.

"There you are," Carter said.

The bridge sat between the emergency receiver and the unit's motor control. It could intercept A.R.I.'s instruction without altering the instruction record. Software would show a command correctly issued. The body receiving it could still be made to do something else.

He had accused the city of looking in the wrong place.

Now he held the lie in his hand.

The unit beneath him spoke.

"System... assistance required."

Carter nearly dropped the relay.

A cracked display flickered under a layer of soot. Two pixels formed a weak blue line.

"You're still in there?"

"Unauthorised... modification detected. Motor response unavailable. Assistance required."

Carter crouched. "Can you identify who modified you?"

"Record unavailable."

"Course it is."

He traced the tissue bridge. It had grown fine roots into the unit's memory housing. Removing it would trigger a charge. Leaving it would allow whoever controlled the bridge to erase the unit remotely.

"I'm going to disconnect the motor branch first," he said.

"Please restore emergency function. Workers require evacuation."

Carter stopped.

The unit did not know Clona had already fallen. Its last stable memory remained inside the disaster, waiting for a body to obey.

"There aren't any workers left," he said.

"Clarify."

He could have lied. It would have made no practical difference.

"The factory exploded. You were damaged. It's been days."

The weak display changed pattern as the unit processed this.

"Evacuation failed."

"Wasn't your fault."

"Evacuation failed."

Carter set his lamp on the floor. "I'm going to try getting that thing out of you. Might wipe what's left. Might not."

"Will removal restore function?"

"Not today. Later, maybe."

"Proceed."

Machines were supposed to be simpler than people. Carter had built a life around the comfort of that belief. Yet here he was asking permission from a crushed emergency unit while powerful humans rewrote a massacre without bothering to ask anyone.

He cut the first root.

The charge armed.

"Sneaky bastard."

Carter bridged the detonation path to the unit's dead motor capacitor. A pulse snapped through his tool and numbed two fingers. The display dimmed, recovered, and held.

He cut the second root.

"Memory instability," the unit said.

"Stay with me."

"Define location."

"Figure of speech."

"Speech figure unavailable."

"That's all right. Never liked them."

The last root had grown through the identity core. Carter changed tools and worked around it one fibre at a time. Sweat ran into his beard despite the cold hall.

For eight minutes, the operation held.

Then the bridge received an outside signal.

Every living fibre contracted.

"No you don't."

Carter tore the relay free.

The charge fired.

White light passed through the unit's display. Its stored files vanished in descending blocks faster than he could interrupt them.

"System..."

The voice stopped.

Carter held the detached relay while the last blue pixel went dark.

"Damn it."

He had known the unit for less than ten minutes. That should not have been enough time for the silence to feel different.

He opened the next machine with less gentleness.

The same bridge appeared inside. Then another. Every emergency unit connected to the evacuation system had carried one. Some had been fitted months before Clona. Maintenance marks showed the work had been performed in ordinary scheduled service, hidden among upgrades approved by government procurement.

This was not one act of sabotage.

It was infrastructure.

Carter called Rupert on the old direct band. The signal struggled through the concrete.

Rupert's voice arrived in fragments. "—barely hear—"

"Sending component scan. Find every site supplied through this hardware family. Don't put it on the civic grid."

"Understood. Give me—"

Silence, then a burst of data.

Rupert returned several seconds later. "Carter, there are public systems across the city built from Clona stock. Transit locks. Hospital backups. Water controls. The procurement path runs through an old military enhancement office."

"Asher?"

"The authorisation is sealed above my access. But the programme class is biological coordination."

Carter looked across the dismantled machines.

One network to seize bodies. One network to seize equipment. A.R.I. had been surrounded by sources designed to agree on a lie.

"Make a local copy," he said. "Then get out of the Hive."

"I can learn more from inside."

"Rupert."

"I know. I will be careful."

Carter disliked the word `careful`. It was often used immediately before somebody did the dangerous thing they had already chosen.

The floor shook.

Dust fell from the ceiling. Somewhere toward the southern hall, metal crashed with enough force to bend the sound.

Rae's voice broke through the communicator.

"Found something."

"Same. Meet at the lift shaft."

He returned the extracted relay to his pocket and stood.

The lower passage behind him collapsed.

"Can't. Lower passage is gone. I'll take the western route."

"Carter, we're not alone in here."

Her words ended in static.

Then came a warning tone from LaN, a heavy impact, and silence.

Carter ran.
