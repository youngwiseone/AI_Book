# Chapter 9: Back to Clona

By the time Rae and Carter returned to Clona, the ruins had been cleaned for cameras.

Government sheeting covered the torn facade. Each white panel carried the council seal and a line about remembrance. The street had been cleared of glass, personal belongings, and the paper notices families had tied to the security fence. A row of fresh memorial lights shone against the rain.

Nobody had repaired the hole in the building.

"They've made it presentable," Carter said.

"That worries you?"

"Presentable machinery is usually hiding the expensive problem."

They watched from the shell of an office across the street. Official search crews had left an hour earlier. Two security drones patrolled the upper perimeter while a human guard remained near the main gate, sheltered from the rain and more interested in his heater than the ruins.

Blade's access authorisation would still work for another twelve minutes. After that, the system would record them as intruders.

Rae adjusted the strap holding LaN against her back. "Main gate?"

Carter looked offended. "We've got valid access."

"For twelve minutes."

"Exactly. Be rude not to use it."

They crossed the road.

The guard scanned their credentials, frowned at the temporary clearance, and looked toward the government sheeting as though hoping the building might settle the matter for him.

"Site is unstable."

"So are we," Carter said.

Rae stepped on his boot before he could continue. "Our instructions came from Blade Nathan. You can refuse us if you give your name and record the refusal against the investigation."

The guard discovered a powerful commitment to administrative neutrality and let them through.

Inside, Clona smelled of wet concrete, burned insulation, scorched coolant, and something sweeter beneath all three. Rae knew the scent from enhancement clinics. Preservative gel. The kind used to keep living tissue viable outside a body.

The factory's public floors had produced civic AI components, pressure engines, and industrial control units. None required preservative gel.

LaN descended from her shoulder and sampled the air. "Trace concentration increases toward the southern service level."

Carter aimed his lamp down the main machine hall. "Emergency units are below us. If somebody forced bad information into A.R.I., that's where the hardware will be."

"I want the records wing and whatever's producing that smell."

He took the flower-painted communicator from his coat and handed it to her. "Direct channel. No grid handshake. Press once to call, twice if something's trying to eat you."

"What does three times mean?"

"You've forgotten how buttons work."

They split at the collapsed lift shaft.

Rae and LaN entered the records wing through a gap where an emergency shutter had stopped half-closed. The shutter's lower edge was bent outward. Fingernail marks striped the paint beneath it.

She did not let herself imagine how long the workers had tried.

The first office had been emptied. Storage wafers, personal terminals, and even paper files were gone. Cleaning marks showed where equipment had rested before the blast.

"Search teams?" LaN asked.

"Search teams label what they take."

In the second office, the removals had been less careful. A cabinet stood open with one hinge torn away. Somebody had pulled the obvious data blocks and overlooked the mechanical duty roster fixed inside the door.

Rae photographed it.

Five technicians had entered the pressure wing during the night before the explosion. Four names belonged to Clona staff killed the next morning. The fifth field contained no name, only a military procurement code.

LaN searched his local archives. "Supplier dissolved eleven years ago. Biological coordination equipment. Wartime exemption classification."

"Enhancers."

"Likely."

Rae followed the preservative smell downstairs.

The corridor beyond had never appeared on Clona's public plan. New walls had been built inside old service space, their surfaces painted to resemble the factory concrete around them. The deception worked until fire cracked the outer layer and revealed clean composite beneath.

Rae found the concealed door by the absence of soot around its seals.

It opened to Blade's authorisation.

"That feels generous," she said.

"Alternative interpretation," LaN replied. "The door's permission database was damaged and has become welcoming."

The room beyond was a laboratory.

Restraint frames lined one wall. Their proportions varied: human, machine, and shapes somewhere between. On the opposite side, alignment rigs surrounded a central cradle built to hold a civic AI unit. Fine tubes ran from biological reservoirs into mechanical interface tools.

The workstations had been wiped, but physical work left physical history. Rae found scratches where a large body had struggled against a frame. Synthetic skin caught in one buckle. Dried blood under a floor rail. Tool impressions in the central cradle matched the access points Carter had shown her in A.R.I.'s data model.

This was not a factory lab repurposed after the disaster.

It was where someone had practised making living bodies and machines answer the same command.

LaN projected a scan over the central rig. "Recent use. Forty-eight to seventy-two hours."

"After Clona."

"Yes."

The investigation team had not simply covered evidence. They had continued the experiment inside the sealed disaster site because nobody else was supposed to enter.

Rae opened the cradle's lower panel. A row of tissue-threaded relays sat behind it, each one carrying two authority structures. The first imitated A.R.I.'s civic permissions. The second used wartime biological coordination code.

She removed one with insulated tools.

The moment it left the socket, every lamp in the laboratory went out.

LaN's screen became their only light.

"Was that you?" Rae asked.

"I aspire to greater theatrical restraint."

Something moved in the corridor.

Rae replaced the relay and drew her weapon. The lights remained dark.

A shadow crossed the doorway, too high and broad to belong to the guard outside. It did not enter. Rae heard weight shift against concrete, followed by a wet mechanical click.

Then silence.

She waited a full minute before approaching the door.

The corridor was empty.

On the floor lay an injector vial, crushed at one end. A military supplier's mark showed beneath streaked blood. Beside it, one enormous footprint darkened the dust.

LaN scanned both. "The blood contains seventeen distinct genetic signatures."

"One person?"

"One sample."

Rae sealed the vial with the relay.

Her communicator clicked.

"Found something," Carter said through static. His voice had lost its usual irritated steadiness. "You need to see it."

"Same. Meet at the lift shaft."

"Can't. Lower passage collapsed after I came through. I'll take the western route."

Rae looked toward the footprint.

"Carter, we're not alone in here."

Only static answered.

Behind her, deeper in the laboratory, metal began to bend.
