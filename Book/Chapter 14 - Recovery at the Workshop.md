# Chapter 14: Old Wounds

Rae woke to the sound of Carter swearing at LaN.

"That limb was already delicate," LaN said.

"That limb was on backwards."

"It had adapted."

"It had been walking in circles."

Rae opened her eyes. The workshop came into focus slowly: lamps hung from patched beams, tools arranged according to a logic only Carter understood, rain silvering the high windows. She lay on a narrow bed beside the office wearing a shirt large enough to contain a smaller person alongside her.

Bandages tightened around her ribs when she breathed.

Carter glanced over. "Alive, then."

"Disappointing somebody, apparently."

He pointed at the small wall screen. The morning report showed their photographs beside footage of the factory collapse. Search operations had been suspended due to structural danger. Both investigators were presumed dead.

"They stopped looking quickly," Rae said.

"Almost like they weren't hoping."

LaN tested his repaired limb. It moved with a slight hitch but remained attached. "Carter used three components not approved by my manufacturer."

"Your manufacturer isn't here."

"This was also your answer when I asked about anaesthetic."

Carter brought Rae tea in a mug with a cracked handle. He did not ask how she felt. He had already checked her bandages twice while pretending to look for tools.

On the main bench, beneath a steel bowl, waited the relay from Clona.

"Show me," she said.

"You can barely sit."

"I don't need my ribs to look."

He helped her to the bench while making it seem as if he were only moving the chair. Under the lamp, living fibre wound through the relay's metal channels in deliberate patterns. Carter pointed out the false input bridge, the physical trigger, and the tiny charge designed to erase the attached AI when anyone removed it incorrectly.

"The unit I opened at Clona died because of this," he said. "Not shut down. Died. Whatever memory it had was burned from the inside."

Rae found Asher's surgical notation beside the procurement mark.

"It's theirs."

Carter waited.

So Rae told him.

Asher had found her after the war in a transport shelter full of children nobody could place. They gave her food, a room, and rules solid enough to push against. They taught her to fight, investigate, and understand the biological technologies the war left scattered across the country.

Back then, Asher's enhanced arm had seemed miraculous.

"They told me strength was for protecting people," Rae said. "They said they'd taken the damage so my generation wouldn't have to."

"And later?"

"Later there was always another war coming. Another enemy. Another reason to keep the programmes alive. Their body started rejecting the changes. Instead of stopping, they replaced whatever failed."

She described the stabilisers. The surgeries. The voices Asher sometimes answered when nobody else was in the room. A network of enhanced soldiers linked through biological control signals, first for coordination in combat and later because Asher no longer knew how to exist without being obeyed.

"The thing in the factory recognised me," Rae said. "It asked me to run while the signal was interrupted."

Carter looked at the relay. "So the machines and the enhancers were controlled through the same hardware."

"Asher spent years saying the disciplines should be reunited. I thought they meant shared research."

"Maybe they meant one set of hands around both throats."

Rae hated how well the sentence fit.

She rose too quickly. Pain folded her against the bench.

Carter caught her before she fell. "Sit down."

"I have to face them."

"Not today."

"They know I'm alive if the creature reported back."

"Then they can enjoy the suspense."

Rae pushed away from him. "You don't understand. If Asher is behind Clona, every hour gives them time to move the network."

"I understand rushing into a machine while it's still running. You lose fingers that way."

"Asher isn't a machine."

"No. Machines usually stop when parts fail."

The cruelty of it silenced her. Carter heard it too. His face tightened.

"Sorry," he said.

Rae lowered herself into the chair.

They made the plan properly. Rae would go to Asher once she could move without reopening her ribs. Carter would take the relay to Rupert. His government access and knowledge of A.R.I.'s architecture might identify how the control bridge reached both machine and enhancer networks.

"You trust him?" Rae asked.

"With my life."

The answer came too quickly to be performance.

LaN climbed onto the bench. "Probability note: statements of absolute trust often precede especially poor decisions."

Carter glared at him. "Did you put that in there?"

Rae managed a painful smile. "He's always been dramatic."

They spent the afternoon separating evidence into copies. Nothing went onto the civic grid. Carter brought out the old peer-to-peer communicator Rae had returned to him, its black casing scratched and dotted with painted flowers.

"Ugly," Rae said.

"Reliable."

"The flowers are nice."

"Rupert's fault."

His thumb rested briefly on a small daisy near the antenna.

By evening, Rae could cross the workshop without help if she moved slowly. Carter packed the relay into a shielded box and checked the old communicator twice.

At the door, he looked back at her.

"No heroic nonsense while I'm gone."

"You're taking all of it with you."

"Good. Keep it that way."

Then he left to meet Rupert, carrying the proof of what had been done to A.R.I. and no idea that he was also carrying the last hours of his son.
