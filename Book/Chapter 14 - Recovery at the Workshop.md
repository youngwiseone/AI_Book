# Chapter 14: Old Wounds

Rae woke to the sound of Carter swearing at LaN.

"That limb was already delicate," LaN said.

"That limb was on backwards."

"It had adapted."

"It had been walking in circles."

Rae opened her eyes. The workshop came into focus slowly: lamps hung from patched beams, tools arranged according to a logic only Carter understood, rain silvering the high windows. She lay on a narrow bed beside the office wearing a shirt large enough to contain a smaller person alongside her.

Bandages tightened around her ribs when she breathed.

Carter glanced over. "Alive, then."

"Disappointing somebody, apparently."

He pointed at the small wall screen. The morning report showed their photographs beside footage of the factory collapse. Search operations had been suspended due to structural danger. Both investigators were presumed dead.

"They stopped looking quickly," Rae said.

"Almost like they weren't hoping."

"Did they find the man in the south hall?"

"Report didn't mention him."

Rae watched until the footage changed to another street. They had not shown the doorway where the loading arm had been.

LaN tested his repaired limb. It moved with a slight hitch but remained attached. "Carter used three components not approved by my manufacturer."

"Your manufacturer isn't here."

"This was also your answer when I asked about anaesthetic."

Carter brought Rae tea in a mug with a cracked handle. He did not ask how she felt. He had already checked her bandages twice while pretending to look for tools.

On the main bench, beneath a steel bowl, waited the relay from Clona.

"Show me," she said.

"You can barely sit."

"I don't need my ribs to look."

He helped her to the bench while making it seem as if he were only moving the chair. Under the lamp, living fibre wound through the relay's metal channels in deliberate patterns. Carter pointed out the false input bridge, the physical trigger, and the tiny charge designed to erase the attached AI when anyone removed it incorrectly.

"The unit I opened at Clona died because of this," he said. "Not shut down. Died. Whatever memory it had was burned from the inside."

Rae found Asher's surgical notation beside the procurement mark.

"It's theirs."

Carter waited.

So Rae told him.

Asher had found her after the war in a transport shelter full of children nobody could place. They gave her food, a room, and rules solid enough to push against.

"I used to take bread from the kitchen," Rae said. "Hide it behind the bed."

Carter kept his hands still beside the relay.

There had been crumbs in the sheets. Rae remembered brushing them out before Asher came to change the bedding, trying to get every one. A hard heel fell from behind the mattress and struck the floor between them.

She waited for them to take it.

Asher picked it up, looked at the green along one edge and put it aside. They left the room. When they came back, they carried a tin that had once held field dressings.

"Lift the lid," they said.

Fresh bread, wrapped in a cloth.

"The old bit's gone off. Use this."

Rae pulled the tin against her stomach. Asher pointed to its latch.

"Shut that properly. Mice can get through more than you'd think."

She had expected another sentence. A question about why she did it, or a rule about taking things. Asher went back to changing the sheets.

"They kept bread in that tin," she told Carter. "Even after I stopped hiding it."

Her thumb rubbed the cracked handle of the mug. The relay lay between them with Asher's mark cut into its side.

"They taught me most of what I know."

Fighting. Investigation. The biological technologies the war left scattered across the country. Back then, Asher's enhanced arm had seemed miraculous.

"They told me strength was for protecting people," Rae said. "They said they'd taken the damage so my generation wouldn't have to."

"And later?"

"Later there was always another war coming. Another enemy. Another reason to keep the programmes alive. Their body started rejecting the changes. Instead of stopping, they replaced whatever failed."

She described the stabilisers. The surgeries. The voices Asher sometimes answered when nobody else was in the room.

"They kept the old coordination programme running," she said. "I thought the work was support. Keeping people stable."

"You saw it?"

"I saw what was happening to Asher."

She knew which filter fitted their regulator. Which tube to check when they couldn't get comfortable in the chair. They would sit still for her to do those things, then start describing the next alteration while she put the tools away.

"The others," Carter said.

Rae put the mug down.

"No. They told me they were helping them."

Carter waited. She wished he would ask something else.

"I went less often," she said. "Kept making sure they had supplies. That was easier."

Carter moved the lamp so she could see the relay without leaning over it.

"If I didn't go, I could pretend they weren't getting worse."

She had still checked whether the deliveries arrived. She had been very thorough about that.

"The man in the factory told me to run," Rae said. "While LaN interrupted the signal. He was trying to help me."

Carter looked at the relay. "So the machines and the enhancers were controlled through the same hardware."

"Asher spent years saying the disciplines should be reunited. I thought they meant shared research."

"Maybe they meant one set of hands around both throats."

Rae hated how well the sentence fit.

She rose too quickly. Pain folded her against the bench.

Carter caught her before she fell. "Sit down."

"I have to face them."

"Not today."

"They know I'm alive if his implant sent anything back."

"Then they can enjoy the suspense."

Rae pushed away from him. "You don't understand. If Asher is behind Clona, every hour gives them time to move the network."

"I understand rushing into a machine while it's still running. You lose fingers that way."

"Asher isn't a machine."

"No. Machines usually stop when parts fail."

The cruelty of it silenced her. Carter heard it too. His face tightened.

"Sorry," he said.

Rae lowered herself into the chair.

They made the plan properly. Carter would take the relay to Rupert that evening. His government access and knowledge of A.R.I.'s architecture might identify how the control bridge reached both machine and enhancer networks. Rae would stay until morning. If she could reach the door without needing the bench to hold her up, they would argue about Asher then.

"I know the house," she said. "I can get in."

"I believe you. It's the getting out I'm worried about."

She sat through the next breath before answering. "First light. No later."

Carter didn't like it. He brought the chair closer anyway.

"You trust him?" Rae asked.

"With my life."

The answer came too quickly to be performance.

LaN climbed onto the bench. "Probability note: statements of absolute trust often precede especially poor decisions."

Carter glared at him. "Did you put that in there?"

Rae managed a painful smile. "He's always been dramatic."

They spent the afternoon separating evidence into copies. Nothing went onto the civic grid. Rae set the plain black field communicator beside LaN. Carter took the flower-painted set out of his own coat and checked its battery.

"Ugly," Rae said.

"Reliable."

"The flowers are nice."

"Rupert's fault."

His thumb rested briefly on a small daisy near the antenna.

He connected Rae's field set to the workshop aerial and brought up the repairers' repeater band. A burst of complaints about a missing pump filled the room.

"Not private," he said, turning it down. "But it doesn't need the civic grid. LaN can put the check-ins in code. Give the relay time to answer before you repeat yourself."

Rae touched the transmit button without pressing it.

"Can someone on this band reach the rescue crews?"

"Probably. Everybody can hear you ask."

She looked at the wall screen. Her photograph was still in the row of presumed dead.

"They stopped searching. He might be alive."

Carter moved the set closer.

She pressed the button. "Message for Clona rescue control. Possible trapped survivor in the passage into the lower disposal hall. By the loading dwarf. Enhanced man, badly injured."

A burst of static answered. Then a voice asked her to repeat the location.

She did.

"Don't send anyone from this band in," Rae said. "The structure was still moving when we left. He was being driven by a control implant. He may not be able to stop himself hurting someone. The crews need to know before they approach."

"Who's reporting?"

Rae kept her thumb on the button. Carter watched the set.

"Someone who got out."

She released the button. For a moment there was only static.

"I'll pass it to rescue control. Can't tell you whether they'll go."

"Ask them to keep it on the search record."

The voice read back the location. Rae corrected the level, waited until it was repeated properly, then lowered the set. No rescue crew had answered.

"What if you don't come back?" Rae asked.

He wrote the frequency on a parts label and stuck it to the set. "Stay here till morning. If you leave, tell me where. No guessing."

LaN sent a test packet. The painted radio clicked against Carter's palm.

Rae drew the entrance hall of Asher's house on the back of a delivery sheet. She marked the room with the support frame, then the training hall beyond it.

"You can stay on my shoulder until we're close enough to see the controls," she told LaN. "Under my coat if they start asking about you."

"No."

Her pen stopped.

"They'll want to know what I've done to you."

"I don't want to be carried into their reach."

"You're still limping."

"Then carry me to the door. Not through it."

LaN crossed the paper, dragging his repaired limb over Rae's line. He stopped beside the entrance she had drawn.

"I want to see the way out. I can't do that from inside your coat."

Rae looked at the torn coat hanging beside the bench. She had been planning how to keep him hidden, not how he would leave.

"I need you where you can record the controls."

"I can move when I see them."

She put the pen down.

"You'll tell me if you can't get a view?"

"Yes."

Rae turned the paper so he could see the route into the next room. She had drawn that doorway too narrow. She widened it before he could point it out.

Late that afternoon, Rae could cross the workshop without help if she moved slowly. Carter packed the relay into a shielded box and checked the old communicator twice.

At the door, he looked back at her.

"No heroic nonsense while I'm gone."

"You're taking all of it with you."

"Good. Keep it that way."

Then he left to meet Rupert. Rae listened to the workshop door close and reached for the field set.
