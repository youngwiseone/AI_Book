# Chapter 2: Rae Gets Pulled In

By the afternoon after Clona, the rain had made the concrete outside the government intake building the same grey as the floor inside.

Rae knew the waiting room from years of debriefings. They'd changed the chairs once and stopped leaving ashtrays out, but still kept the same kind of magazines. The one next to her promised to tell her what her boss really thought of her. She left it closed. Water darkened the shoulders of her coat. Every time the vent came on, the smell of beer-flavoured vape drifted back across the room. Rae pulled her wet coat away from the back of her neck. LaN crouched by her chair on four slim limbs, his white shell the cleanest thing there.

"Your pulse is elevated," LaN said.

"Not sure what this is all about."

"Statistically, this room does very little for morale."

Rae let out a small laugh at that.

The summons had come without explanation, only a priority code usually reserved for bomb threats, missing council members, or crimes the government preferred to name before the evidence did. She had already been waiting more than an hour. Typical. Catastrophe for everyone else. Delay for the people tasked with cleaning it up.

She stretched the stiffness from her shoulders and set her empty coffee mug on the scarred side table beside her. Blue light from LaN's display glanced off the ceramic and danced across the ceiling. For a moment it reminded her of sun on salt water, and of the coast near the town she had not visited in years.

"They are ready for you now, in room one," LaN said.

"Miracles do happen."

She stayed where she was. Room one could wait a moment. She watched the blue reflection tremble across the ceiling, enjoying the thought of someone on the other side of the doors checking the time for a change.

LaN turned his screen towards her.

"Yes, all right."

She held out her arm. He climbed onto her shoulder, adjusted his grip and thanked her. Rae waited until he was settled before getting up.

The receptionist looked up from a call as they passed. Rae gave her a small wave; the woman returned it with two fingers still holding her pen. LaN flashed their credentials, and the inner doors opened onto a long hallway that lit itself segment by segment as they walked.

The walls were lined with framed photographs, donation plaques, polished certificates, and propaganda posters stamped with the slogan: `Pursuing quality with a human touch.`

Rae disliked that line on principle. LaN tucked his limbs in to let her straighten the shoulder of her coat. There was a great deal of work in those joints and about four seconds in the name she'd given him.

On this side of the doors there was dark wood on the walls and nothing in the ceiling sounded like a fan with a bad bearing. At the end of the corridor, a bright gold `1` had been fixed to a heavy office door. It hadn't quite covered the holes from the last number.

She knocked once out of habit, then opened it without waiting.

The first thing that hit her was the smell of whiskey.

The second was Blade Nathan.

He stood behind the desk like a soldier who had never really left the war behind. Tall, broad, and kept upright by force of will more than comfort, Blade wore his modifications the way some men wore ceremonial armour: not proudly, exactly, but without apology. The left hand resting on the desk ended in dark hooked talons. Fine scale-work shimmered faintly under the skin near his throat. Even standing still, he gave the impression of a body engaged in constant negotiation with itself.

He smiled at her reaction. It was not a pleasant expression.

Rae made herself return a thinner version of it and sat only when his open hand invited her to do so.

"As much as it pains me to arrange a meeting in person," Blade said, voice low and dry, "there are matters I would prefer discussed somewhere more secure than the public channels. I do not wish to keep you longer than necessary."

He opened a drawer, withdrew a syringe, and drove it into his own arm with the bored efficiency of long habit.

Rae flinched despite herself.

Blade gave a short, humourless snort. The empty vial sailed across the room and struck a dartboard on the far wall, where it joined several others already embedded there like trophies from a private war. Up close, the signs of strain were impossible to miss: blistering at the edge of the collar, a faint tremor in the shoulder, the rigid care with which he lowered himself back into the chair.

Bio-enhancers had taught Rae early that power loved soft language. `Adjustment`. `Optimisation`. `Necessary risk`. Then came the hidden bruises, the pills, the shaking hands when the medication wore thin.

A light blinked on the corner of Blade's desk. He silenced it. It blinked again.

"What?"

A woman's voice answered. "Recovery stores. The ward's stabiliser delivery is still on hold."

"How much have they got?"

"Enough for tonight. Less if they take more transfers."

Blade glanced towards the dartboard.

"The replacement contract?"

"Emergency approval could go through this afternoon. I can't promise it."

"Can the current supplier fill the order?"

"They have it ready. But the programme renewal includes the other equipment allocations. Review asked for those to be separated."

He opened the renewal on the desk.

"Send the delivery under the existing arrangement. I'll sign the continuation."

"All of it?"

"For this week."

"I can send the separate application as well."

"Do that. Get the delivery moving first."

He signed.

"Tell the ward when it's on the vehicle. Not when somebody says it's approved."

The connection closed.

Rae looked at the document. "Is the equipment urgent too?"

Blade closed the file. "We can argue about the rest when the ward has its delivery."

He flicked a holographic screen to life between them.

Clona filled the air in cold blue light.

The footage showed the factory rupturing from the inside, steel peeling outward, steam and flame swallowing the frame. Rae had imagined the blast since hearing about it. Seeing it made her stomach knot anyway.

"You may have heard the public version of the Clona disaster," Blade said while she watched. "We made sure not to include one detail. An automated decision chain may be responsible."

Rae looked up sharply. "A.R.I.?"

Blade's gaze did not move from the projection. "According to the machine's own report, multiple emergency procedures failed to trigger. The source data around the event is cloudy at best, which is unlike any record tied to her systems."

"Back a little," Rae said.

Blade moved the footage back.

"Before the steam."

The factory loading entrance appeared through a gap between two vehicles. A shutter descended across it. Somebody on the inside struck the lower edge before it met the floor.

Rae held up a hand. Blade stopped the image.

"That closed."

"Pressure isolation," he said. "An automatic response."

"Was it supposed to close?"

"That is one of the questions."

"Then don't give me a list of things that failed to happen. I need the things that did happen too."

LaN leaned towards the projection. "The camera angle does not show the other exits."

"I know," Rae said. "Keep the frame reference. Not a conclusion."

He marked it on his display.

Seven years of A.R.I.'s oversight had made the city cleaner, safer, harder to manipulate by ordinary rumour and bureaucratic failure. That did not tell Rae who had closed this door. She waited until Blade moved his hand away from the playback controls.

"And you want me to do what?" she asked.

"Look into the matter before the story settles into something none of us can undo."

"You will have access to A.R.I. directly," Blade added.

"And the factory's human side?"

"Staff records are being assembled."

"Contractors. Inspections. Who approved the equipment."

Blade glanced towards the closed drawer. His fingers tightened against the desk.

"Those records belong to several offices."

"Including yours?"

"Yes."

"Then put that request through to all of them."

"You have not accepted the assignment."

"I haven't heard all of it yet."

Blade's jaw tightened. He opened a new field beside the case number and entered the request. Rae waited for him to finish.

"That does not guarantee release," he said.

"No. Leave the unanswered ones in the record."

He turned the screen so she could read it. The request included his office. Its status was pending.

LaN saved the reference beside the frame number.

Rae sat back. "Now I'll talk to A.R.I."

Blade pushed the drawer shut with his hip. Across the room he keyed open what Rae had taken for a cupboard, tapping in the number with the tip of one talon.

A lift waited on the other side. Its mirror had a cloudy patch at shoulder height.

"Come along, then."

LaN made his own copy of the pending request as they stepped in.
