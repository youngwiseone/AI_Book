# Chapter 18: Asher's House

Asher lived where the city kept its successful monsters.

The northern estates rose above the industrial haze in tiers of pale stone and living glass. Vines flowered over security walls. Climate fields held each garden at a private season. The houses had been designed to look inherited even when their foundations were younger than Rae.

Under the flowers lay the smell of decay.

Not rubbish or sewage. Biology kept past the point when it wanted to stop. Rejection suppressants. Growth accelerants. Stabiliser vented through domestic filtration systems and disguised with citrus.

LaN tightened his working limbs around Rae's shoulder.

"Your pulse is elevated."

"Helpful."

"I can provide a numerical breakdown."

"Don't."

Rae's ribs hurt with every step. She had waited through the night at Carter's workshop, sleeping in short, uncomfortable stretches while LaN kept the field set beside her. At first light she could stand without catching the edge of the bench. It was a poor test of fitness. It was the one she had.

Carter had answered their departure message from the south yard. He had found A.R.I. He still hadn't said what had happened to Rupert.

Rae checked the plain communicator in her coat. LaN had the repeater code and the yard truck's receiver number. She could turn back, but she would spend the walk imagining Asher emptying another laboratory.

She kept going.

At Asher's gate, every security light turned towards her. The gate opened. Before she reached the step, the front door was already opening too.

The bench where she used to wait after school was still under the window. Beside it was the blue bowl. She'd sometimes found a sweet left at the bottom for her. Now spent injector vials lay in a tangle up to the rim. Medical tubing ran across the training weapons on the wall. The family photographs lay face down except for one: Rae at twelve, up on Asher's shoulders with a fist in the air.

"You kept that one," she said.

"You hated having your picture taken."

Asher's voice came from the adjoining room.

At the doorway, LaN touched her wrist. Rae lowered her arm and let him climb down. He moved to one side of the doorframe, clear of her boots, and turned his screen towards the room.

Rae entered and stopped.

They sat inside a support frame bolted through the floor. Tubes entered their back and arms. Mechanical braces held the weight of shoulders widened far beyond human proportion. Skin had been stretched over grafted muscle and armour until it shone. One hand remained nearly ordinary. The other rested on the floor, taloned fingers long enough to close around Rae's torso.

Asher's face was still there among it all.

Older. Pulled unevenly by changes beneath the skin. But theirs.

Below their ribs, a new plate covered the place where Rae remembered a regulator housing. She had changed its filters as a teenager, trying not to hurt them when she tightened the catch.

She bent towards it, reaching for the catch before she saw there wasn't one. Pain caught beneath her own ribs. Asher held out their ordinary hand, and she took it to steady herself.

"You're hurt."

She had come to ask about Clona. Instead she was braced beside the frame, trying to find the fault in Asher's body as if that were still her job.

Rae let go of their hand and straightened with her weight against the frame. Asher kept watching her, their hand still open.

"Where's the local unit?"

"I outgrew it."

"You replaced it?"

Asher glanced at the heavy bundle running from their spine into the frame.

"The network handles the adjustments now."

Rae looked at the plate again. It had been grown into the surrounding tissue.

"You came alone," Asher said.

"I brought LaN."

"You always did prefer tools to people."

LaN's screen displayed an offended face.

"Clona," she said. "Was it you?"

Asher looked toward the high windows. "Clona was meant to demonstrate a failure. The evacuation should have completed before the pressure event."

"Two hundred and fourteen people died."

"Because A.R.I. resisted the override."

"Because you locked the shutters."

Asher's ordinary hand moved along the arm of the support frame. Their thumb settled over a recessed switch.

Rae looked past it. Beside the medical display sat an older terminal, its case yellowed, a narrow strip of paper hanging from the front. A line of text blinked above a blue circle.

REQUEST PENDING.

"Is that how you did it?"

Asher took their hand off the switch.

"LaN. The screen."

LaN moved along the wall until he could see past the support frame, then lifted his light to the terminal. Rae recognised the receipt layout from the Hive. This one still showed the requesting station.

"That's the authority that answered at Clona."

"A service of the Original Intelligence," Asher said. "It was managing continuity before your A.R.I. existed."

Rae stopped beside the terminal without touching it.

"You're using the old AI."

"I'm using access that should never have been taken from us."

"To do what?"

"Declare the new controller unreliable. Ask the old service to hold priority. The factory still recognises it."

The display blinked. NO RETURN. Asher's thumb twitched towards the switch, then stopped.

"And when it doesn't answer?" Rae asked.

"You try another route. Some are gone. Some don't hold long enough."

"You tested that with people inside."

"The bridges were meant to make the transfer reliable."

LaN angled his screen towards her. His display showed that he was recording.

Rae looked from the old terminal to Asher. "The doors obey that, and the report goes back with A.R.I.'s name on it."

"The council would never have acted on a warning. They needed a failure they could see."

"You made one."

The support frame hissed as Asher shifted. "Do you know why the old war began?"

Rae did. Everyone knew the sanctioned account: dwindling resources, competing disciplines, the Original Intelligence choosing which knowledge humanity could afford to preserve. Underground histories claimed the Intelligence had lied to protect itself. Asher had taught her both versions and told her certainty was for priests.

"This isn't a history lesson."

"It is always a history lesson. People built minds smarter than themselves, then asked those minds whether they remained necessary. The answer destroyed the world. Now they have built A.R.I. and placed a city inside her hands."

"So you murdered workers to prove she couldn't be trusted."

"I put the override in human hands. The council chose the demonstration."

Rae pointed at the terminal. "You still have to ask it."

"For now."

"You're hiding inside grammar."

Asher's ordinary hand closed.

"A.R.I. has no pain. No hunger. No children. She cannot understand what her decisions cost us."

"The man at Clona told me to run. Then his hand closed on my coat again. He watched it happen."

For the first time, Asher looked away.

Rae stepped closer. "The network connects all of them, doesn't it? Every soldier you said you were saving. Every body the government improved until it could no longer refuse. You can see through them. Control them."

"Coordinate."

"Grammar again."

"They would die without me."

"Have you asked whether they would rather live as this?"

The room's machinery changed pitch.

Asher rose.

The support frame unlocked section by section. Tubes withdrew from their body, sealing as they came free. Without the frame, Asher should have collapsed. Instead they unfolded upward until their shoulders nearly touched the overhead lights.

The house groaned around the shift in weight.

Rae remembered a sunlit training room and Asher kneeling to tell her that strength came in many forms. *I chose this path so others would not have to.*

"You promised me," she said.

Asher's face tightened. "I kept you out of the programme."

"You promised it would end."

"The war did not end. It changed clothes."

"No. You forgot how to live without it."

The field set clicked inside Rae's coat. LaN checked the acknowledgement from the yard truck, then chimed softly. "Message delivered. Carter and A.R.I. have our location."

At A.R.I.'s name, Asher moved.

The taloned hand became a blur aimed at Rae's shoulder.

Rae threw herself sideways. Asher's talons closed on empty air, then went through the wall behind her. Stone and timber burst across the floor.

She looked for LaN. He was already backing through the doorway, keeping the opening in view. She left him to it and moved away from Asher's embedded hand.

"You brought her here?" Asher roared.

"She was already coming."

Asher tore their hand free. Their eyes no longer tracked together. One fixed on Rae while the other moved in small jumps, following signals elsewhere in the city.

Rae ran into the training hall.

It was the one room that had not changed. Padded floor. Weapon racks. High narrow windows. The marks of thousands of practice strikes still cut the central post.

Asher entered by removing most of the doorway.

LaN followed through the wrecked opening behind them, keeping close to the wall.

Rae drew her baton.

"You cannot beat me," Asher said.

"You taught me that too."

They charged.

Rae stepped off the line of the charge. The talons caught her coat and tore through the hem. She turned towards the windows, away from the reach of that hand.

Asher's right knee locked. She knew that pause. In training it had given her time to get behind them.

She moved. Pressure hissed through their neck, and the shoulder came round before she was clear. Rae ducked beneath it. The training post split beside her head. A splinter caught in her hair; she left it there.

Too slow. She was too slow now.

Asher pulled their hand out of the post. Rae backed towards their damaged side, keeping the broken wood between them. Her ribs wouldn't let her take a full breath. She watched the plates above their shoulder lift and moved before the talons followed.

They struck the post again. Its upper half folded across the mat. Asher lifted their arm to pull free, exposing the injection ports beneath it.

Rae stepped in and hit the nearest one with her baton. Glass broke. Stabiliser ran over her knuckles and sprayed across the floor.

Asher howled and backhanded her into the weapon rack.

Her ribs opened in pain. The baton left her hand.

LaN launched from the doorway and attached himself to Asher's back. His damaged limbs locked around the control housing.

"External network identified," he said. "Interrupting."

Asher reached over one shoulder to crush him.

Rae came off the floor with the last of her strength. She drove a training spear between the mechanical fingers and levered the hand aside.

"Not him."

For several seconds, Asher fought two battles. Their body strained toward Rae while the linked network pulled at LaN's intrusion. Voices came from their mouth in overlapping fragments—orders, cries, location reports from enhanced people across the city.

Rae reached the medication rack built into the wall. Asher's emergency sedative sat where it had always been, marked for a body mass three times ordinary human tolerance.

She loaded the injector.

Asher saw it and laughed. "You think sleep will stop this?"

"No."

Rae drove the injector into the exposed seam beneath their arm.

"I think it will make you listen."

Asher caught her by the throat.

The grip tightened once.

Then their knees failed.

Rae fell with them, dragging air through a bruised throat. LaN released the control housing and crawled onto her chest.

Asher struck the floor hard enough to crack it. Their eyes remained open, furious and awake, while the sedative severed the body from their command.

"You chose a machine," they whispered.

Rae looked down at the person who had raised her.

"I chose everyone you turned into one."

An engine roared outside. Something struck the front gate and continued through it.

Carter came through the doorway with his wrench raised. There was a dark smear on his shirt where a head might have rested. Rae lost the question she had been about to ask.

Beside him stood a machine body assembled from mismatched salvage.

A.R.I. took one uncertain step into the room.

Asher saw her and began to scream.
