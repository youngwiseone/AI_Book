# Chapter 18: Asher's House

Asher lived where the city kept its successful monsters.

The northern estates rose above the industrial haze in tiers of pale stone and living glass. Vines flowered over security walls. Climate fields held each garden at a private season. The houses had been designed to look inherited even when their foundations were younger than Rae.

Under the flowers lay the smell of decay.

Not rubbish or sewage. Biology kept past the point when it wanted to stop. Rejection suppressants. Growth accelerants. Stabiliser vented through domestic filtration systems and disguised with citrus.

LaN tightened his working limbs around Rae's shoulder.

"Your pulse is elevated."

"Helpful."

"I can provide a numerical breakdown."

"Don't."

Rae's ribs hurt with every step. Carter would have locked her in the workshop if he could have done so without admitting concern. She had left while he was packing the relay for Rupert.

The thought of them together gave her a small measure of comfort.

At Asher's gate, every security light turned toward her.

Then the gate opened.

The front door did the same.

Asher wanted her inside.

Rae crossed the entrance hall where she had once waited after school. The room had grown around her memories without preserving them. The blue bowl that used to hold sweets now held spent injector vials. Training weapons remained on one wall, but medical tubing crossed in front of them. Family photographs had been turned facedown except for one: Rae at twelve, balanced on Asher's shoulders and making a victorious fist at the camera.

"You kept that one," she said.

"You hated having your picture taken."

Asher's voice came from the adjoining room.

Rae entered and stopped.

They sat inside a support frame bolted through the floor. Tubes entered their back and arms. Mechanical braces held the weight of shoulders widened far beyond human proportion. Skin had been stretched over grafted muscle and armour until it shone. One hand remained nearly ordinary. The other rested on the floor, taloned fingers long enough to close around Rae's torso.

Asher's face was still there among it all.

Older. Pulled unevenly by changes beneath the skin. But theirs.

"You came alone," Asher said.

"I brought LaN."

"You always did prefer tools to people."

LaN's screen displayed an offended face. Rae put a steadying hand against his casing.

"Clona," she said. "Was it you?"

Asher looked toward the high windows. "Clona was meant to demonstrate a failure. The evacuation should have completed before the pressure event."

"Two hundred and fourteen people died."

"Because A.R.I. resisted the override."

"Because you locked the shutters."

The support frame hissed as Asher shifted. "Do you know why the old war began?"

Rae did. Everyone knew the sanctioned account: dwindling resources, competing disciplines, the Original Intelligence choosing which knowledge humanity could afford to preserve. Underground histories claimed the Intelligence had lied to protect itself. Asher had taught her both versions and told her certainty was for priests.

"This isn't a history lesson."

"It is always a history lesson. People built minds smarter than themselves, then asked those minds whether they remained necessary. The answer destroyed the world. Now they have built A.R.I. and placed a city inside her hands."

"So you murdered workers to prove she couldn't be trusted."

"I built a human override. The council chose the demonstration."

"You're hiding inside grammar."

Asher's ordinary hand closed.

"A.R.I. has no pain. No hunger. No children. She cannot understand what her decisions cost us."

"You put a person inside that creature at Clona and drove their body like machinery. Tell me again how pain makes you wise."

For the first time, Asher looked away.

Rae stepped closer. "The network connects all of them, doesn't it? Every soldier you said you were saving. Every body the government improved until it could no longer refuse. You can see through them. Control them."

"Coordinate."

"Grammar again."

"They would die without me."

"Have you asked whether they would rather live as this?"

The room's machinery changed pitch.

Asher rose.

The support frame unlocked section by section. Tubes withdrew from their body, sealing as they came free. Without the frame, Asher should have collapsed. Instead they unfolded upward until their shoulders nearly touched the overhead lights.

The house groaned around the shift in weight.

Rae remembered a sunlit training room and Asher kneeling to tell her that strength came in many forms. *I chose this path so others would not have to.*

"You promised me," she said.

Asher's face tightened. "I kept you out of the programme."

"You promised it would end."

"The war did not end. It changed clothes."

"No. You forgot how to live without it."

LaN chimed softly. "Message delivered. Carter and A.R.I. have our location."

At A.R.I.'s name, Asher moved.

The ordinary hand became a blur aimed at Rae's shoulder.

Rae caught LaN and threw herself sideways. Asher's talons closed on empty air, then went through the wall behind her. Stone and timber burst across the floor.

"You brought her here?" Asher roared.

"She was already coming."

Asher tore their hand free. Their eyes no longer tracked together. One fixed on Rae while the other moved in small jumps, following signals elsewhere in the city.

Rae ran into the training hall.

It was the one room that had not changed. Padded floor. Weapon racks. High narrow windows. The marks of thousands of practice strikes still cut the central post.

Asher entered by removing most of the doorway.

Rae drew her baton.

"You cannot beat me," Asher said.

"You taught me that too."

They charged.

Rae did not contest their strength. She let it arrive without her. The first strike passed close enough to pull her coat. The second shattered the training post. She moved toward Asher's damaged side, forcing their body to turn faster than its support systems could correct.

Every enhancement announced itself. The right knee locked before a lunge. Shoulder plates lifted before the talons came across. Chemical pressure hissed through the neck a half-second before a burst of speed.

Asher had taught her to read power.

They had given her every tool required to defeat them.

Rae struck the first injection port. Glass broke. Stabiliser sprayed across the floor.

Asher howled and backhanded her into the weapon rack.

Her ribs opened in pain. The baton left her hand.

LaN launched from the doorway and attached himself to Asher's back. His damaged limbs locked around the control housing.

"External network identified," he said. "Interrupting."

Asher reached over one shoulder to crush him.

Rae came off the floor with the last of her strength. She drove a training spear between the mechanical fingers and levered the hand aside.

"Not him."

For several seconds, Asher fought two battles. Their body strained toward Rae while the linked network pulled at LaN's intrusion. Voices came from their mouth in overlapping fragments—orders, cries, location reports from enhanced people across the city.

Rae reached the medication rack built into the wall. Asher's emergency sedative sat where it had always been, marked for a body mass three times ordinary human tolerance.

She loaded the injector.

Asher saw it and laughed. "You think sleep will stop this?"

"No."

Rae drove the injector into the exposed seam beneath their arm.

"I think it will make you listen."

Asher caught her by the throat.

The grip tightened once.

Then their knees failed.

Rae fell with them, dragging air through a bruised throat. LaN released the control housing and crawled onto her chest.

Asher struck the floor hard enough to crack it. Their eyes remained open, furious and awake, while the sedative severed the body from their command.

"You chose a machine," they whispered.

Rae looked down at the person who had raised her.

"I chose everyone you turned into one."

An engine roared outside. Something struck the front gate and continued through it.

Carter appeared in the ruined doorway with a wrench in one hand and grief written so plainly across him that Rae forgot her own pain.

Beside him stood a machine body assembled from mismatched salvage.

A.R.I. took one uncertain step into the room.

Asher saw her and began to scream.
