# Pass 37: Cooperation without endorsement

## Development choice
Following the instruction to develop legitimate opposition surviving A.R.I.'s exoneration, the older placard-holder already present in Chapter 8 becomes a recurring residents' contact, Tom Vale. He is a development-branch character interpretation, not a newly supplied author biography or an additional conspirator. The bereaved sister keeps her own purpose; the investigator remains independent.

## Chapter 8
Goal: Rae tests what lies behind an opponent's demand to shut A.R.I. down. Turn: the man can reject an unsupported account of Clona while still opposing her civic authority. Cost: evidence will not automatically produce the coalition Rae wants. Hook: he asks for findings, and she keeps his contact and review reference.

His grievance is concrete: night freight rerouted past bedrooms and appeals producing the same impersonal answer. It is a complaint to investigate, not narrator proof of malicious AI. He keeps his original notice and allows a copy. No new state-wide law, atrocity, movement hierarchy or historical date is established.

## Chapter 20
Goal: Rae seeks help getting the corroborated evidence heard outside the council's account. Turn: Tom agrees to seek independent records and report what they show, but will not endorse reinstatement. Rae explicitly challenges that position; he asks where a fourth appeal would go if the system returned unchanged. She still thinks the city needs A.R.I., and cannot supply a credible answer. Cost: Rae commits to questions at a residents' meeting without promising an outcome she cannot deliver. A.R.I. asks that her actual statement be included, rather than insisting that vindication entitles her to govern.

This is civic cooperation and political disagreement, distinct from Nessa's right not to give a personal trauma account. Tom does not become an admirer, a secret operative or the spokesman for all opponents. Patient files remain sealed.

## Chapter 21
A short midday follow-through keeps the agreement active. The residents have published the exonerating finding but retain their objections. Rae adds the newly received archive decision to the issues she must explain; Tom does not magically know a private decision made that morning. Their recurring scrutiny helps ground public review without claiming one meeting created the entire new civic arrangement.

Placed beside the archive handover, not inside the final bench/grief sequence. The quiet ending and locked outcomes remain unchanged. No additional exposition about the old AI, reconstruction ancestry or war chronology.
