# Pass 18: The care Rae remembers

## Scene intention before revision
Chapter 14: Rae wants to identify Asher's work without losing the person who raised her; the relay's mark forces her to place remembered care beside present harm; admitting both costs her the simpler account of a monster she never loved; the need to confront Asher becomes personal as well as investigative.

## Change
Expanded the existing account of shelter, food and upbringing into a brief memory. Rae hides bread behind her bed. Asher replaces a spoiled piece with fresh bread in a reused dressing tin and gives a practical instruction about mice rather than shaming or interrogating her. Carter listens without interpreting it for her.

The scene returns to the marked relay before Rae continues. Asher's care was real; so is the evidence. Neither cancels the other. This prepares the emotional difficulty of Rae's confrontation and grief without supplying an excuse for coercion.

## Canon and limits
The bread and tin are new branch proposals. The already-established post-war shelter remains, but no age, year, ancestry, reconstruction method or new war chronology is assigned. No new claim is made about why Rae was in the shelter.

The tin is a contained memory detail, not a new mystery object requiring a later discovery. No possession is moved into the current house or workshop. Asher's control, responsibility for Clona and eventual death are unchanged.

## Review focus
Keep the memory particular and proportionate, without a diagnosis, sentimental absolution or a perfect-parent speech. The practical response should feel like someone Rae could genuinely have trusted.

Independent review found no blocker in viewpoint, chronology or physical continuity. Kept the return to mug and relay without adding a moral conclusion about Asher. Compared with the manuscript’s family scenes: remembered care stays practical and Carter does not explain Rae’s feelings for her.
