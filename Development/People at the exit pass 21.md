# Pass 21: People at the half-closed exit

## Scene intention before revision
Prologue: workers want a routine shift and then a way out; the release command does not free the plant and shutters close; Len and other workers help one another through the remaining gap while pressure rises; the blue circle and blast end their ability to control what happens.

## Changes
Replaced the distant hive/beacon opening with a worker encountering an ordinary, already-signposted maintenance problem. A vibration interrupts that task before the larger failure becomes visible. The damaged trolley is not a cause of the disaster.

The existing half-closed exit now has people acting at it. Len frees a trapped boot; the woman who gets through turns to pull others out; another worker helps people lower themselves under the shutter. Cooperation briefly matters even when they cannot stop the catastrophe.

Removed the claim that pressure has made human hands irrelevant. Their actions affect who can get out, without defeating the override or promising rescue for everyone.

## Canon and limits
Len and the immediate actions are new branch proposals. His survival or death is not established, and no family, ancestry, enhancement history or later role is assigned. The twenty-six outer-lane escapees and the later casualty totals remain unchanged; this scene does not add a separate counted group.

The override text, uncommanded authority, blue circle, blast, cordon and public misattribution remain. The maintenance dwarf still acts at the manifold. The opening does not identify Asher or attribute the old AI's present conscious intention.

## Review focus
Check exit geometry, agency without impossible strength, scene-to-wide-view transition, and whether the named worker creates a human anchor without falsely promising a new protagonist.

Independent review found no blocker in geometry, scale or the transition back to the wide disaster view. Len’s fate remains deliberately unresolved and should be checked in the next whole-manuscript continuity pass; the name does not establish a promised return. Cooperation, not a lone hero, makes the escape possible.
