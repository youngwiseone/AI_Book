# Pass 31: What Rae knew

## Scene intention
Chapter 14: Rae must connect the physical evidence to the person who raised her without treating their explanations as verified facts; Carter asks what she actually witnessed; the captive's resisted commands at Clona distinguish what she now knows from what she previously believed.

## Change
Replaced the retrospective assertion that Asher continued the network because they could not exist without obedience. In this early account it made Rae sound as if she already knew the extent of the coercion. She now distinguishes the old coordination programme from the later support work she thought it had become. She had observed Asher's deterioration, not independently inspected what was being done to everyone else.

The existing account of the captive telling her to run remains the concrete new evidence. Asher's dependence on control is still confronted and demonstrated in Chapters 18–19; the change does not remove that character motive, only stop Rae presenting the later conclusion as old knowledge.

## Scope and review
No new incident, complicity, occupation, date, ancestry or medical procedure is added. Her prior belief is a branch interpretation consistent with the existing shared-research explanation. The recovery plan and disagreement about confronting Asher are unchanged. Independent developmental review identified this as a genuine knowledge-state problem and advised against adding another answers-versus-evidence argument. That additional scene was not retained.

Final review found the distinction clear: misplaced trust, not prior knowledge of atrocities. Net manuscript addition: 24 words.
