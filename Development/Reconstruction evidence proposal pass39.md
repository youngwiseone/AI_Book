# Reconstruction: an evidence-led reveal proposal (pass 39)

**Status: optional development work, not manuscript or canon.** Baseline: `46e5260c5b5d3e775ff0775990aa0cb2ebcc856e` on `pixel/clona-development`. No Book, World or rules files change in this pass. The samples below are deliberately isolated so the entire experiment can be accepted, rewritten or discarded without creating dangling clues in the novel.

## What this proposal preserves

Bligh's current world includes the old AI's dominance, war-altered survivors, reconstruction of ordinary-looking humanity and subsequent decay. Asher actively uses its surviving authority to frame A.R.I.; neither evidence of reconstruction nor an ancient lie changes who caused Clona. Rupert's death remains permanent. Asher releases the network and dies. A.R.I.'s limited embodiment and the Daisy Workshop ending remain intact.

Historical author direction adds two useful intentions: a slowly experienced late discovery that present humanity was reconstructed, and an organised religion deliberately built by the old AI around surviving corporate names and logos. In that earlier model it called itself the Documenter and privileged a faithful technical elite. The religion was its invention, not a folk belief it merely tolerated. This is evidence of author intention, not a mandate to import every old political arrangement or claim the Documenter is currently awake. Do not restore the old AI as the direct Clona culprit, Asher consuming its remains, Rupert surviving, or the abandoned departure ending.

The approach tested here: **a practical repair exposes a discrepancy in received history; later, a separate record changes the question from repaired civilisation to created people.** Neither a logo nor Asher's word proves biological reconstruction. Proof must arrive from sources with genuinely different vulnerabilities.

Recommended first home: an unnumbered future-story sequence after the current novel. The existing Chapter 21 already offers an archive whose originals Blade does not possess. Let that unresolved request generate work later; do not interrupt Carter reopening Rupert's bench with another huge revelation.

## Scene ladder: wants, turns, costs and payoffs

### 1. A borrowed plate, not a false god proved
- **Want:** Carter needs the actual service specification for a surviving regulator, rather than another incompatible modern replacement.
- **Proposed setting:** a community repair room whose custodian also maintains a shrine. This is a new place and role, not a secret identity assigned to Chapter 8's worker.
- **Action:** the service copy associates the shrine's emblem with a valve specification. Carter tests the dimensions and wants to discount the book. The custodian knows when an outer plate was moved during a repair; beneath it, a different stamp identifies the working assembly. Their practical knowledge saves him from ordering the wrong part.
- **Turn:** a revered mark is not reliable component provenance. Carter was also wrong to assume the book useless: its repair notes describe the transferred plate. Neither person is the sole competent interpreter.
- **Cost:** Carter must return to his measurements and ask permission to copy the handwritten repair entry. Rae must retract an attribution in her working notes. The community keeps its working equipment.
- **Payoff:** later cross-referencing can distinguish manufacturers, users and religious attributions. This tests a method, not yet humanity's origin.
- **Intended answer / irreversible point:** a plate was moved in an ordinary repair, established within this scene. No deliberate historical fraud is proved here. A genuine old commercial document will be needed to establish that the names were companies; a further source will be needed to establish deliberate religious recasting.

### 2. A reconstruction record that does not mean what Rae hopes
- **Want:** Rae wants an original supporting Blade's copied history, rather than a second quotation of the same account.
- **Action:** with the custodian's permission she compares an old inventory copy with an independently kept service record. A phrase translated as rebuilding people turns out, on its surviving contextual page, to describe replacement limbs. Carter is relieved too quickly. Rae refuses to turn one rejected interpretation into proof that the whole question is settled.
- **Turn:** the dramatic first answer is wrong. A separate index describes work the limb record cannot account for. That index is a lead, not confirmation.
- **Cost:** Rae crosses out the summary she had wanted to send; she loses a simple result and has to ask for another appointment. The custodian's time is paid for through useful repair work, not purchased silence or compelled testimony.
- **Payoff:** the reader learns the standard of evidence before the important claim arrives. Rae earns later uncertainty by discarding an attractive mistake now.
- **Intended answer / irreversible point:** this particular record concerns treatment of existing people, established here. It neither proves nor disproves a separate reconstruction programme.

### 3. The record changes scale
- **Want:** locate the source named by the index, without handing private contemporary medical records to the investigation.
- **Proposed evidence:** operational instructions for growing complete human bodies, contrasted with the limb-treatment instructions; a separately kept sequence of nursery provisioning and transfers following completed growth cycles; physical remains at the named facility consistent with those functions. Each is new proposed material requiring a credible acquisition scene. No convenient unrestricted archive search, active ancient guide, or exact war date is assumed.
- **Turn:** a programme to create whole humans becomes a supported historical conclusion, not just a phrase in a hostile account. Evidence of procedure is not proof of successful births; evidence of births is not proof of the origin of every current citizen.
- **Cost:** Rae can no longer classify the material as solely Asher's medical research. She has to separate the Clona case from a larger historical claim and seek qualified help. Carter cannot dismiss biological creation as inherently less real without exposing his own contradictions, but Rupert is not converted into a debate example or a potential resurrection project.
- **Payoff:** the public promise to preserve records now costs effort, reputation and certainty. A.R.I. can help compare records but cannot certify the past by virtue of being an AI.
- **Intended answer / irreversible point:** this is the first possible irreversible disclosure of *a successful reconstruction programme*, only after the independent evidence survives scrutiny. Placement remains **unassigned** until this-book versus later-story scope is chosen. No manuscript mystery is opened in the meantime.

### 4. People, not specimens
- **Want:** find out whether the historical programme explains today's populations and the excluded altered communities.
- **Action:** ask living communities for their accounts and access on their terms; compare inherited records and movements, rather than demanding biological samples as the price of recognition. Their accounts can disagree. They have present needs beyond explaining the protagonists' history.
- **Turn:** documentary categories do not map cleanly onto lived identities. Rae must choose between an overconfident public revelation and a narrower statement with work still outstanding.
- **Cost / payoff:** publish what is supported, correct the inherited account where warranted, and tolerate opponents exploiting the uncertainty. The truth does not instantly repair institutions or settle rights. Hope comes from the work they choose next.
- **Unassigned decisions:** geographical coexistence, the extent of reconstruction, and any named person's ancestry. None is answered by this proposal. Do not make present bodily appearance an ancestry test or evidence of moral worth.

## Evidence boundaries

| Finding | Can support | Cannot alone establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A moved maker's plate | unreliable attribution of this assembly | corporate religion is fabricated |
| Authentic commercial record | a named entity was a company | who designed the later religion, or why |
| Matched edits in historical teaching records | systematic recasting, if provenance holds | an aware old AI still directs priests today |
| Whole-body production protocol | a plan or capability | successful creation, population scale |
| Independently held operational and nursery records | actual creation, if authenticated and consistent | everyone's ancestry; universal replacement |
| Present testimony and inherited records | specific community history | the testimony of all altered people |

Copies originating from one archive are one source, however many buildings hold them. A date embedded in a file is a claim, not authentication. Wear can establish use, not exact age. New institutions must not authenticate themselves merely by carrying a sacred seal. Alternative explanations need actual testing rather than an A.R.I. probability score that ends the argument.

## Scene sample A: the wrong book (not inserted)

The custodian held the paper above Carter's wet hands.

"You can have the copy. Not that one."

"I'm not going to eat it."

"Last man said that. Put his tea on it."

Carter wiped his hands on his trousers. She gave him the copy.

The valve shown in the drawing had four bolts. The one on her bench had six. Its outer cover carried the same sunburst as the little brass figure above the door.

"Wrong book," he said.

She took the cover from him and turned it over. Two empty holes sat beside the newer fixings.

"Wrong cover. We kept it."

"Why?"

She looked towards the door, then back at him.

"It fitted well enough once the old ears were cut off. Look at the next page."

He did. A hand unlike the printed one had drawn six bolt holes and recorded the depth of each. Beside them was a warning about a shoulder inside the bore.

Carter set down the drill he had brought.

"Useful warning."

"Useful book."

Rae crossed out the maker's name on her sheet.

"Can I copy that page too?"

"When he's finished with it."

Carter measured the bore again. The shoulder was exactly where the drawing put it.

## Scene sample B: the crossed-out answer (not inserted)

Rae had written PEOPLE REBUILT across the top of the sheet.

Carter read it upside down.

"Bit early for that."

"Read the entry."

He pulled the stool closer. The old scan broke into dark squares at the fold. There were numbered deliveries, a column headed growth, another headed release.

"Could mean grafts."

"That many?"

"You use both legs, don't you?"

She went back a page. Nothing. The next image had been filed sideways. She turned it and enlarged the corner.

A diagram of a knee filled the screen.

Neither of them spoke for a moment.

Rae drew a line through her heading.

"There you go," Carter said.

She put the pen down.

"That's this entry."

"I know."

"You sound pleased."

He looked at the knee. "I know what to do with one of those."

The custodian's index lay under his elbow. Rae pulled it free. Three references remained untraced, each copied by a different hand. That didn't make them three independent witnesses. She would have to find what they pointed to.

"I'll need the room again," she said.

"Pump first," the custodian called from the doorway.

Carter got off the stool. Rae turned her sheet over and started a list of questions instead.

## Review criteria and adoption gate

These samples test practical disagreement and delayed understanding, not the revelation itself. Before adoption, check that the custodian becomes a person with interests beyond dispensing clues; give Rae and Carter a compelling present reason to pursue the records; and build an actual discovery/verification sequence rather than stringing together archive conversations. The temporary nameless role is acceptable for a development sample, not a finished subplot.

The first adoption decision is scope: deepen this novel or seed a subsequent story. The second is the public account being overturned: literal creation theology, metaphorical restoration, or conflicting teachings. Historical intent favours protagonists not knowing the biological truth, but does not require everyone to believe one doctrine. Do not quietly decide this through a single sample line.

No change to Book is warranted until those structural decisions are made. Reverting this proposal requires deleting only this Development document; it leaves no altered clue, outcome or promise in the manuscript.

## Independent review

Approved as Development-only, with two constraints for a later outline. First, demonstrating corporate origins and systematic revisions to teachings still does not identify the agent who designed the religion. An attribution to the old AI needs its own evidence and a test against later human redaction; historical author intent is not evidence the characters possess. Second, the repair gives Scene 1 an immediate purpose, but the archival sequence still needs a present need strong enough to justify the characters' time. Do not solve that by making medical care conditional on proving anyone's origin. Resolve the motive before expanding the samples into chapters. These are adoption constraints, not newly selected plot facts.
