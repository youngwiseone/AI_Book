# Reconstruction discovery: three structural approaches and a scene sequence

**Development proposal only.** No Book, World or rules changes. Baseline: `2114eba8804151d594355010550650ece42fb8f1`. The samples are not newly established events, places, people or evidence. The author has specified reconstruction in the world and historically wanted a slow late discovery; he has not expressly reselected the old Asher-revelation scene for this version.

## Recommendation
Use a complete second movement if reconstruction is to be discovered within this novel. Do not hide the question in an extra Clona clue or cram its answer into Asher's surrender. The current plot resolves bodily coercion; the larger discovery asks who was permitted to preserve and name human history. These can converge in the workshop's work without making the Original AI Clona's true culprit.

The strongest present motive is a repair the group has already agreed to undertake, not a historian sending them on a treasure hunt. The repair exposes a discrepancy; after that, the historical question must become an explicitly chosen undertaking, with time and obligations the group cannot pretend are free. Care must not depend on proving anybody's ancestry.

## Placement A: a substantial aftermath movement before the existing ending
**Closest to the older near-ending intent.** Keep00–20 and the residents' meeting intact. After the meeting, but before the six-month workshop scene, allow several scenes over an unspecified period. The workshop is being established rather than already complete. An agreed community repair gives the group a practical route to surviving originals omitted from Asher's copied research.

What it needs: roughly 4–6 full scenes, potentially 6,000–9,000 words, not a target to pad to. The current final chapter would become unwieldy; this option needs author approval for new chapter/part divisions before integration. Its major cost is delaying the emotional ending and asking the reader to accept a second discovery arc after the main antagonist's death. The new arc cannot merely escalate to another boss.

What it preserves: permanent deaths; Asher's responsibility; the residents' unresolved grievance; all final workshop actions, including Carter using Rupert's bench. The six-month archive delivery must be reconciled: either the earlier source comes from an independent institution, or Blade's material arrives earlier and his later visit has another truthful purpose. Do not duplicate the same archive delivery to manufacture two sources.

## Placement B: braid a secondary investigation into the existing middle
**Best chance of a single integrated novel, highest revision cost.** An inherited component's provenance must obstruct a necessary Clona inference; its source later contains the wider history. This requires rebuilding the factory-to-procurement chain, broadening the access/recovery timetable and preserving urgency without a fake countdown. It cannot be added to08's gate encounter: that worker already has a concrete job and no reason to deliver this evidence.

The revealed history must not prove Asher's action for the protagonists or excuse it. Its factual discovery can precede18, allowing Rae to challenge Asher's justification without accepting their authority as historian. But19 still resolves the immediate controlled-body crisis, not an ideological debate about everyone's origins.

Before adopting, decide which current evidence step becomes genuinely insufficient and why the new acquisition is necessary. If it merely supplies additional confirmation of what10/15 already establish, abandon this route. Do not stretch the plot by making a previously readable code inexplicably unreadable.

## Placement C: first movement of a subsequent story
**Least disruptive and my present preference for quality.** Leave this novel's ending exactly where it is. The archive received in21 and the request for originals motivate later work. The workshop supplies relationships, ongoing obligations and a reason people come to them; it does not become a secret resistance headquarters overnight.

The revelation can still be slow and consequential, but it would not fulfil the earlier preference for discovery near the end of this particular book. That tradeoff should be named plainly. A later book can give living altered communities interests beyond answering the protagonists' questions and can explore the decaying system's reach without turning it into a universal villain.

## Proposed sequence, usable in A or C
All supporting particulars below are proposals, not recovered canon.

### 1. The repair that produces the question
A community uses an inherited building for bathing, laundry and winter shelter. Its lower water circuit is failing. The component index in a surviving research copy points to this building's service book; the group seeks that original because it may supply specifications needed for an agreed repair, not because a random repair happens to uncover the whole lost history. Route C can use the archive already delivered in21. Route A must openly establish an independent earlier source or change that delivery's timing, as discussed above. A custodian/maintainer, Miri, wants the agreed repair completed, not her institution investigated. Carter's modern service drawing identifies a cover, not the working assembly; Miri's handwritten repair history corrects him. This repays the provenance premise without making faith equivalent to incompetence.

Their immediate repair succeeds provisionally. During the work Rae finds an old service index whose `restoration` section matches a phrase in Asher's copied research. Miri allows a copy after recording what was taken. She does not hand over a secret archive because the protagonists are important. No reconstruction conclusion yet.

**Cost:** a further visit, access on Miri's terms, and unfinished maintenance obligations. **Irreversible local answer:** the cover was moved; the working assembly's source differs from the shrine's attribution. That does not prove the religion fraudulent.

### 2. The attractive answer fails
The first restored-body record concerns a knee. Rae retracts her exciting interpretation before anyone publishes it. Carter's relief is personal: a repair is something he knows how to understand. A separate index item cannot be explained by the knee protocol, but it remains a lead.

**Cost:** time lost and an embarrassing correction recorded, not erased. **Purpose:** demonstrate the method once, not subject every later paragraph to a courtroom hearing. Use the existing pass39 sample as a starting point, not another identical scene.

### 3. Physical access carries a present obligation
The index points to a disused lower annex of the same building. Miri permits inspection during scheduled work; she needs the active laundry line protected. Carter must isolate the correct branch, not cut an old pipe simply because a new diagram calls it redundant. A.R.I. cannot certify what lies beyond a wall from a modern floor plan.

They encounter intact parts of a full-body growth installation and its physical maintenance marks. Rae can distinguish whole-body assembly from prosthetic fabrication, but apparatus and instructions prove capability or intent, not successful births. No sleeping human, speaking ancient guide or conveniently powered intelligence is needed.

**Cost:** they finish the protective repair before taking more measurements; exploration waits when present users need water. **Turn:** the question is no longer merely a mistranslated word. Trial A tests this return visit after the knee interpretation has failed. The cover discrepancy is already known; the renewed obligation is another leaking joint, not repeating the same provenance discovery.

### 4. A different institution supplies the human outcome
A separately maintained receiving-house ledger records living infants taken from the named facility, with correspondence about feeding, placement and actual carers. This is not another printout from the same service archive. Its keeper decides what can be copied; individual entries need not be released publicly. A second expert examines the synthesis protocol and the correspondence before Rae states a conclusion.

Taken together, the protocol, installed apparatus and independently kept receiving records support a historical programme that created whole living humans from preserved biological material. None alone proves it. The finding does not establish every current person's ancestry or eliminate surviving altered humans. The responsible, emotionally substantial conclusion is that reconstruction actually happened, not that everybody in the room has been classified.

**Cost:** the protagonists choose to pursue a larger historical claim rather than treating it as a solved case. They cannot expose identifiable living families merely to make the revelation more dramatic.

### 5. Attribution and historical scale require a further discovery
The first conclusion is only that this programme produced living humans. To establish the author's fuller background, the characters still need operational records attributable to the Original Intelligence's administration, independently contextualised as a post-war restoration programme, and evidence of its reach. A logo, a matching modern key, the number of tanks or an AI claiming authorship cannot settle these alone. This stage needs its own acquisition, challenge and scene of interpretation before anyone says the old AI rebuilt humanity. It does not require making the old AI currently awake or classifying every present person. These attribution scenes are not written in the trials below.

### 6. The result changes a relationship before a public argument
Let Rae react before anybody delivers the moral. She has treated Asher's account of protection as both a lie and part of her own upbringing. Evidence of the human-production programme does not vindicate Asher's present coercion or prove their fear of AI justified. It makes the inherited account harder to dismiss wholesale while admitting very different interpretations. Only the additional attribution and context described above could support the wider reconstruction claim. Carter initially reaches for a technical label, then finds it cannot settle who the created people were to each other. A.R.I. must not become the authoritative voice on a past she did not witness.

The group states what they can support and what they cannot. No compulsory ancestry test, universal creation category or genetic obedience claim. They must still decide what to publish, where to hear other accounts and how to keep the work from becoming another monopoly over explanation.

## Religion is a separate claim
Commercial origins, religious reuse and deliberate design by the old AI are three claims with different evidence requirements. A corporate service slogan is not proof of who wrote a later religion. Historical author intent establishes the world's background, not what this group knows. This sequence should not also solve institutional theology unless additional scenes earn it.

## Adoption decisions
1. Is the reader to discover reconstruction in this volume or the next? A and C cannot both be treated as settled.
2. For A, may the post-climax movement receive new divisions, or is the existing short ending more important?
3. What broad account do Rae and other ordinary citizens currently believe? Historic user dialogue supports ignorance of literal reconstruction, but not a uniform doctrine across every community.

None requires fixing a war date, a named person's ancestry or the old AI's present consciousness.
